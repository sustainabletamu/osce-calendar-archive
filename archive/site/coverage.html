How to interpret the archive

Public visibility has limits

Successful retrieval proves what the endpoint returned at capture. It cannot establish coverage of deleted, hidden, unpublished, or inaccessible records, or recover every earlier revision of event copy and media.

The snapshot can change

Later publications and edits require another capture. Months marked “Future at capture” may gain records. Empty historical months remain uncertain rather than being filled with guessed events.

Dates and events have different grains

A multi-day event may produce one calendar row per display day. Weekly instances with different source IDs remain separate records. Occurrence totals are not necessarily counts of independent sessions.

Missing ends remain unknown

No end time was inferred. Page metadata sometimes supplies a multi-day span or repeats the start as the end; those values were not substituted for null occurrence ends. Original timestamps and offsets remain preserved.

Image quality is verified within public access

Image bytes, MIME types, dimensions, and checksums were verified. Full-size public copies are not proven original camera files or original-upload bytes. Supplied captions and alternative text are retained without invented credits.

External destinations need internet

Outbound websites, linked PDFs, forms, and online meetings are preserved as links; their full contents are not archived. JSON and local images remain the source of truth for the gallery and spreadsheet views.
